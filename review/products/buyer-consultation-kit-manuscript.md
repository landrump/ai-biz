# The Buyer Consultation Kit

AI-assisted workflow for real estate agents preparing for buyer consultations,
service-value conversations, agreement walkthroughs, objections, and follow-up.

## Important note

This kit is a practical workflow and drafting aid. It is not legal, tax,
accounting, brokerage, MLS, NAR, or compliance advice. Review all language with
your broker, attorney, MLS, state association, and compliance resources before
using it with clients.

## Quick start

Use this kit before a buyer consultation to prepare:

1. A clear meeting agenda.
2. Your buyer-service value explanation.
3. Buyer agreement talking points.
4. Compensation conversation language.
5. Objection responses.
6. Follow-up emails.
7. A next-step checklist.

## What you need before using the workflow

Collect:

- Your brokerage buyer consultation process.
- Your state or brokerage buyer representation forms.
- Your compensation policy and approved language.
- Your buyer intake notes.
- Your local MLS or association guidance.
- Your preferred buyer consultation format.

## Step 2: Prepare the consultation agenda

Use this structure:

1. Welcome and goals.
2. Buyer situation and timeline.
3. How the buying process works.
4. How you help before, during, and after showings.
5. Buyer agreement and representation discussion.
6. Compensation and cost conversation.
7. Search strategy and next steps.
8. Questions.

### AI prompt

```text
Act as a real estate operations assistant. Create a buyer consultation agenda
for a real estate agent meeting with [buyer profile].

Context:
- Market: [city/area]
- Buyer timeline: [timeline]
- Buyer experience level: [first-time/repeat/investor]
- Main concerns: [concerns]

Requirements:
- Keep the agenda clear and client-friendly.
- Include time for buyer agreement and compensation discussion.
- Avoid legal advice or definitive claims.
- Use a professional, helpful tone.
```

## Step 3: Explain your value

Buyers need to understand what you do beyond opening doors.

### Value categories

- Search strategy.
- Property evaluation.
- Showing coordination.
- Offer strategy.
- Negotiation support.
- Timeline management.
- Inspection and repair coordination.
- Communication with lender, title, and other parties.
- Local market context.
- Problem-solving through closing.

### Value statement template

```text
My role is to help you make a more informed, organized, and confident purchase
decision. That includes helping you understand the market, evaluate properties,
prepare offers, coordinate the process, and avoid preventable surprises along
the way.
```

### AI prompt

```text
- Avoid guaranteed outcomes or legal claims.
```

## Step 4: Buyer agreement walkthrough

Use your official brokerage/state form. This kit helps you prepare talking
points, not replace the form.

### Plain-language walkthrough structure

1. What the agreement is.
2. Why it is used before touring homes.
3. What services are covered.
4. How compensation is addressed.
5. What the buyer should review carefully.
6. Where the buyer can ask questions.
7. What happens next if they are comfortable proceeding.

### Buyer-friendly explanation

```text
Before we tour homes together, we need to make sure the working relationship is
clear in writing. The agreement explains the services I provide, how the
relationship works, and how compensation is handled. I want you to understand it
before signing, so we will walk through it section by section and you can ask
questions before deciding how you want to proceed.
```

## Step 5: Compensation conversation framework

Do not improvise compensation language. Review with your broker first.

### Framework

1. State that compensation is part of the written agreement.
2. Explain that fees and payment structure should be clear before touring.
3. Explain available options only if approved by your broker.
4. Avoid implying that compensation is fixed, standard, or guaranteed.
5. Encourage buyer questions.

### Draft language

```text
Part of this conversation is making sure compensation is clear before we begin
touring homes. The agreement will explain how compensation is structured and
what options may be available. Compensation is not set by law, and the details
should be reviewed carefully so you understand what you are agreeing to before
we move forward.
```

## Step 6: Objection-response bank

### 1. "Why do I need to sign something before seeing a house?"

```text
The written agreement is meant to make the relationship clear before we tour
homes. It outlines the services being provided and how the working relationship
and compensation are handled. My goal is to make sure you understand it before
we take the next step.
```

### 2. "Does this mean I have to pay you out of pocket?"

```text
It depends on the agreement, the property, and the terms that are negotiated.
The important thing is that the compensation structure is clear before we tour
homes, and that you understand the options and obligations before signing.
```

### 3. "Can we just look at one house first?"

```text
Before touring, we need the required agreement in place. We can keep the
conversation focused and make sure you understand the agreement before deciding
whether to move forward.
```

### 4. "Is this negotiable?"

```text
Compensation is not set by law. We can review the agreement and discuss the
terms so you understand what is being proposed and what options may be available.
```

### 5. "I do not want to be locked in."

```text
That is a fair concern. We can review the length, scope, and terms of the
agreement so you know exactly what it covers and what choices you have.
```

### 6. "The listing agent can show it to me for free."

```text
You may have options for seeing a property, but representation and access are
not the same thing. My role is to help you evaluate the property, understand the
process, prepare strategy, and navigate the transaction with your interests in
mind, subject to the agreement we review.
```

### 7. "Can the seller pay your compensation?"

```text
In some transactions, compensation may be negotiated as part of the offer or
handled in another approved way. It depends on the property, seller, agreement,
and applicable rules. We will discuss the structure clearly before you make
decisions.
```

### 8. "What if I find a house myself?"

```text
That is a good question to review in the agreement. The agreement should explain
the scope of the relationship and what happens in different purchase scenarios.
```

### 9. "Can I think about it?"

```text
Yes. You should understand what you are signing. I am happy to answer questions,
and you can review the agreement before deciding whether to move forward.
```

### 10. "This feels like extra paperwork."

```text
I understand. The purpose is to make the relationship, services, and
compensation clear up front so there are fewer surprises later.
```

### 11. "I already know what I want."

```text
That is helpful. My role can still include evaluating fit, identifying risks,
coordinating the process, preparing offer strategy, and helping you move through
the transaction efficiently.
```

### 12. "Can you discount your fee?"

```text
Compensation terms can be discussed before the agreement is signed. I can walk
through what is included in my service and what options may be available under
brokerage policy.
```

## Step 7: Follow-up emails

- [Goal 1]
- [Goal 2]

carefully and sending over any questions before we move forward.

Best,

[Agent Name]

### Email 2: Buyer agreement follow-up

Subject: Buyer agreement next steps

Hi [First Name],

I wanted to follow up with the buyer agreement information we discussed.

Please review the agreement carefully, especially the sections covering services,
term, scope, and compensation. If anything is unclear, send me your questions
and I can help walk through them or point you to the right brokerage resource.

Once you are comfortable with next steps, we can move forward with [search setup
/ showing plan / consultation follow-up].

Best,

[Agent Name]

### Email 3: Not-ready buyer follow-up

Subject: Helpful next steps when you are ready

Hi [First Name],

Thanks again for the conversation. If now is not the right time to move forward,
that is completely fine.

When you are ready, the most useful next steps are:

1. Confirm your budget and financing path.
2. Clarify your target areas and timeline.
3. Review how buyer representation and compensation work.
4. Decide whether you want support before touring homes.

If questions come up, feel free to reply here.

Best,

[Agent Name]

## Final review checklist

Before using with clients:

- Confirm all form references match your brokerage and state forms.
- Confirm all compensation language is approved.
- Remove any language that sounds like legal advice.
- Remove any promise of savings, acceptance, or outcome.
- Confirm the timeline and scope of representation are accurate.
- Keep a copy of buyer questions and follow-up.
